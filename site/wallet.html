Get a Bitcoin wallet

New to Bitcoin? You need one thing to use notzero: a receive address. Here's how to get one — in plain language, no jargon.

What a wallet actually is

A wallet is a free app that holds your bitcoin and gives you a receive address — a string that starts with bc1. Think of the address like an email address for money: you can share it, and people (or in this case, a block reward) can send bitcoin to it.

You control it — not us. notzero is non-custodial: it never holds your coins or your keys. All it ever needs is your public receive address, so a winning block can pay out to you.

Get one in three steps

  1. 1. Install a self-custody wallet — one where you hold the keys (not an exchange). See the options below.
  2. 2. Write down its recovery phrase. The wallet shows you 12–24 words when you set it up. That phrase is your money — it's the only way to restore it. Write it on paper and store it somewhere safe.
  3. 3. Copy your receive address (look for “Receive” in the wallet) and paste it into notzero's setup. Done.

⚠ The one rule that matters

Never share your recovery phrase (seed words) with anyone, and never type it into a website — including notzero. We only ever want your receive address (the bc1… one), which is public and safe to paste anywhere. Anyone who gets your recovery phrase can take your coins. No real service will ever ask for it.

Which kind of wallet?

  • Mobile / desktop wallet (software) — free, takes a couple of minutes, great to start. Perfect for this.
  • Hardware wallet — a small physical device (~$50–150). Overkill to begin, but if the near-impossible happens and you win a real reward, this is where you'd want it to live.
  • An exchange account is not the same thing. On most exchanges the company holds your keys. It works as an address, but you don't truly control the coins — and for a potential once-in-a-lifetime prize, self-custody is the whole point.

Why notzero needs an address

notzero mines a real Bitcoin block on your computer — one lottery ticket per block. If you ever win, the reward is paid in the block itself, straight to the address you set. If you leave it blank, any win goes to the developer's address instead, and a confirmed reward can't be moved afterward. So set your own — it's worth the two minutes.

Bitcoin is self-custody by design: your keys, your coins, your responsibility. notzero never holds either. Back to getnotzero.com.
